What R2H Is

No jargon. No downloads.
No brochures.

Three things that make R2H different from everything else you'll be handed at discharge.

Origin

Built by people who lived this.

R2H didn't come from a boardroom. It came from the ward, the discharge day, and the months after that nobody warned you about — built by people who were there.

Access

Any device. No download.

Works on the phone in your pocket, the tablet at the nurses' station, or the old laptop at home. Nothing to install, update, or remember to update.

Purpose

Yours for as long as you need it.

R2H is built to support your transition — not to keep you dependent on it. When you're settled and ready to move on, it supports that too. No pressure either way.

The information was always there. The structure wasn't. R2H puts it in one place, in plain language, at the moment you actually need it — and then gets out of the way.

Why It Matters

What R2H actually gives you.

The gap between leaving rehab and feeling stable at home is real, dangerous, and almost entirely unsupported. R2H is built specifically for that gap.

01 · Foundation Plan

Your document. Built before you leave.

Before discharge, you build a Foundation Plan — your routines, your care needs, your goals, your contacts. It belongs to you. Not the clinical team. Not the system. You take it home.

02 · Discharge Checklist

Every category. Every gap named.

Equipment, home modifications, carer rostering, funding and support coordination, medications, appointments. Every item tells you what to do if it isn't sorted — and who to call. Nothing left as your problem to figure out alone.

03 · First Week Guidance

Specific, honest, three things a day.

The first week home is the hardest part. R2H gives you daily guidance that names what that week actually feels like — and tells you the next concrete step. Not inspiration. The next step.

04 · Emergency Contacts

Always visible. One tap. No menus.

000. Royal Talbot ward direct. SCIS. Your GP. Your carer agency. Permanently on screen throughout Stage 3 — not inside a menu you have to navigate at 2am when something has gone wrong.

05 · Community Reintegration

Scaffolding that reduces as you stabilise.

Stage 4 progressively hands off to long-term resources — peer programs, community services, ongoing support. R2H gets lighter as you get steadier. That's the design, not an accident.

06 · Access

Free. No download. Any device.

Stages 1–3 are free. No app store. Works on the phone in your pocket, the tablet at the nurses' station, the old laptop at home. Nothing to install before you need it.

On your terms

Stay as long as you need. Leave when you're ready.

R2H is built to support your transition, not create another dependency. Use it as a resource and reference for as long as it's useful. When the routines are holding, the supports are in place, and you know who to call — you decide what comes next. That's always been the point.

Who It's For

This is written directly to you.

Not 'patients and families in general.' You, specifically, wherever you're sitting right now.

SCI Patient

You didn't ask for this roadmap.

But here you are, reading discharge paperwork in a hospital bed while people talk around you. R2H is written like you're in the room, because you are. It won't pretend this is simple, but it will tell you what's actually next.

Family or Carer

Nobody briefed you either.

You're figuring this out in real time, often while also managing everyone else's feelings about it. R2H gives you clear, honest information about what home care actually looks like — without assuming you already know the system.

Peer Worker

You've been here. That's why you're here.

Lived experience is R2H's foundation. If you work in peer support, R2H is designed to sit alongside you — not replace you. Something to hand someone when you can't be there at 2am on a Tuesday.

Clinical Staff

You know what falls through the gaps.

Discharge planning is complex and the follow-through is often underfunded. R2H doesn't try to do your job — it tries to help patients and families hold onto what you've already told them, once the ward is behind them.

This wasn't built by a product team.
It was built from Ground Zero.

R2H was originated by people who left Royal Talbot Rehabilitation Centre, navigated the transition home, and returned as peer coaches at the same institution. The problem R2H solves is not inferred from research. It is known from living it.

Every design decision — what the tool says, what it never says, what is always one tap away and what is permanently on screen — comes from the lived experience of that transition. Not a hypothesis about it.
